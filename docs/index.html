Glossary

Total
Best successful squat + bench press + deadlift at one meet.
Raw
Lifting without supportive suits or bench shirts (belt, knee sleeves and wrist wraps only). Equipped lifters move much more weight, so they are excluded.
Tested
The federation drug-tests its athletes (e.g. USAPL, AMP, USPA Drug Tested).
Full meet (SBD)
The lifter competed in all three lifts, not a bench-only or deadlift-only event.
Bomb-out
Missing all three attempts on one lift. That lift counts as zero, so there is no valid total.
DQ
Disqualified: removed from the results, for example for a failed drug test or rule violation. OpenPowerlifting also marks bomb-outs as DQ.
Weight class
IPF classes. Men: 59, 66, 74, 83, 93, 105, 120, 120+ kg. Women: 47, 52, 57, 63, 69, 76, 84, 84+ kg.
Percentile
"85th percentile" means you beat 85% of lifters in your group, each at their best.

The scoring formulas

Bigger people lift more, so raw totals can't compare a 60 kg lifter with a 120 kg lifter. Every formula divides your total by an expected total for your bodyweight curve, then multiplies by a constant:

score = Total × K ÷ f(bodyweight)

Wilks 1994

f is a 5th-degree polynomial in bodyweight fitted on elite results from the 1990s, when few women competed. K = 500. The classic, though many federations have since moved to newer formulas.

Dots 2019

Developed by Tim Konertz as a modern update of Wilks. f is a 4th-degree polynomial, K = 500. Widely used by federations worldwide, though some have started moving away from it.

IPF GL 2020

The IPF's "Goodlift" points, its official scoring standard: f = A − B·e−C·BW, a curve that rises and levels off. K = 100, so around 100 is elite international level. Not given below 35 kg bodyweight, same as on OpenPowerlifting.

N-Score PLStats

A new, experimental formula made for this project. f = a·BWb, with a separate curve for men and women, and the top 1% of lifters score above 500. With the other three formulas, heavier women score lower on average than lighter women; with N-Score they score about the same. It's updated every week with new data, so your score can change slightly; the version and date are shown next to it.
